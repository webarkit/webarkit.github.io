We are excited to announce a major milestone for our community: the WebARKit website has been completely rewritten and modernized. For this new phase, we chose a cutting-edge tech stack based on Astro and Starlight, a decision driven by an ambitious vision and big projects we have in store for the future.

Among the most noticeable additions is the new section dedicated to [library documentation](/docs/), a fundamental piece that fills an important gap. Now, you can finally consult detailed guides for established libraries like [jsartoolkitNFT](https://github.com/webarkit/jsartoolkitNFT), [ARnft](https://github.com/webarkit/ARnft), and [jsfeatNext](https://github.com/webarkit/jsfeatNext), as well as projects in active development like [webarkit](https://github.com/webarkit/webarkit), [purecv](https://github.com/webarkit/purecv), and [WebARKitLib-rs](https://github.com/webarkit/WebARKitLib-rs). The page already offers practical tutorials (for [ARnft](/docs/tutorials/arnft-threejs/) and [jsartoolkitNFT](/docs/getting-started/first-app/)), and we intend to enrich it further with additional examples. In the coming weeks, we will also integrate API reference pages for each library.

The [online examples](/examples/) section has also been renewed: we removed obsolete content and updated existing examples to their latest versions to ensure a seamless testing experience.

The most exciting highlight, however, is the introduction of the [Studio section](/#studio), dedicated to [webarkit-studio](https://github.com/webarkit/webarkit-studio). Although still in an embryonic state, this tool promises to radically simplify the creation of WebAR applications thanks to a node-based interface, making development incredibly intuitive and immediate.

Looking to tomorrow, what does the future hold for WebARKit? We invite you to follow the evolution of [webarkit](https://github.com/webarkit/webarkit) and [nft-tracker](https://github.com/webarkit/webarkit/tree/master/packages/nft-tracker): for now, they are prototypes, but we are working hard to turn them into concrete reality. Furthermore, if you are looking for the Rust version of [WebARKitLib](https://github.com/webarkit/WebARKitLib), you can explore [WebARKitLib-rs](https://github.com/webarkit/WebARKitLib-rs); if you need an alternative to OpenCV written in Rust with WASM bindings, you can try [purecv](https://github.com/webarkit/purecv), which is used internally by [WebARKitLib-rs](https://github.com/webarkit/WebARKitLib-rs). Please keep in mind that these projects are currently WIP (Work In Progress): the code may undergo radical changes and there might be hidden bugs. Happy browsing!
